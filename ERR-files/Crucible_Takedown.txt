<b>Round 1</b>
By now this challenge has become something of a mainstay so who are we to deny you your fun?. Your very first challenge as newly inducted Survivor Season 7 contestants is to design three cards, each part of the same limited archetype.

We’re keeping things simple for S7’s start so we are only looking for:
One (1) two or three colored uncommon card.
Two (2) monocolored (color, not color identity) common cards, each of a different color matching your uncommon.

As usual, we want your three cards to feel as if they are from the same set so we’re equally looking for aesthetic/thematic cohesion.

<i>I was really trying to overachieve so i actually wrote my own rubric for this one</i>

Balance
Are the commons at an acceptable power level for common? Would you actually want them in this deck? Are they an effect a deck might want multiple of?
Is the uncommon at an acceptable power level for uncommon? Does it pull you into its deck? Does it encourage the synergy it's meant to encourage?
Is anything going to break constructed?
Prompt 
Are there two commons and one uncommon of the colors requested? Is the artist credit, mechanic credit, numbering, etc. correct?
Could this archetype work? Has the designer shown an understanding of this deck's strengths, weaknesses, and desires?
Is the archetype novel? Does it contain something that will play in a new way?
Gameplay
Would these cards lead to fun games of limited? Do they avoid common gameplay issues? 
Would this deck be fun to play? Would it be fun to draft? Would it be fun to play against?
Do the cards have a clear purpose and role in their deck?
Flavour
Do these three cards look like they came from the same set? Do I get a sense of what the set is like tonally?
Is the flavour text concise, clear, polished, and evocative? Do the names match the art and flavour text?
Does the art look good? Does it match the mechanics?
Elegance
Are the cards at an appropriate level of comprehension and boardstate complexity for their rarity?
Are the cards as simple as they could possibly be? Have they done what they do in a clear manner?
Are there clear synergies between the cards that emerge from their mechanics in a not-heavy-handed way?
Syntax
Are the rules and syntax fully correct and standard?
Have any potential rules corner cases and odd play patterns been addressed?
Is reminder text for any new mechanics concise and clear?

<i>I sent a message in the public chat after most rounds explaining my general thoughts</i>

General Comments:
- I'm glad to see that most of you took the repeated warnings bout complexity to heart and submitted cards without extraneous elements or excessive lines of text. Good job.
- There were a lot of commons that missed the mark on power level. The band of acceptable power level for commons is not that wide; vanilla 2 mana 2/2 is too weak, vanilla 2 mana 3/3 is too much. This will keep being a key marking criterion on commons intended for draftable formats.
- I see a lot of talk about people "playing it safe" and I'm not sure what that means. Entries with new mechanics or without, in well-trodden or novel space, on canon planes or new ones, with very simple cards or slightly less simple ones, all got high grades or low grades depending on their quality. I don't think there's a safe approach; if you think there's an easy and risk-free way to get a good grade, you may be underestimatng our standards.
- There are some great flavour-text writers in here and it bumped a few entries up a grade that they managed to be evocative in the space available.

<i>I resumed the public post-results spiel this round</i>

This round
- Constructed decks are not just about explicit synergy but implicit synergy. 2 mana ramp goes with 4 drops, instants go with other instants, aggressive creatures go with burn that hits face, that sort of thing
- Some of you don't know what "Aggro" and "Tempo" and "Control" mean. Aggro decks don't want slow 4 drops and card advantage enchantments. Control decks don't want card disadvantage interaction. Tempo decks don't want to tap out on turn 3 for a card that generates incremental value
- Standard decks of all eras need to do something powerful by turn 4. A lot of entries had cards that theoretically synergize but not in a way where you can curve out and achieve that synergy in a reasonable timeframe

<b>Round 5</b>
You will each be designing the partial contents of a commander precon consisting of 4 cards in total:
- One mythic rare three color legendary creature as the face commander for your Commander Precon.

<i>Public post-results spiel</i>

This round
- In the previous rounds, we've let nine-liners and generally overcomplicated cards survive because of other entries with more egregious errors. That's going to stop around now.
- When designing for an eternal format, novelty is even more important. There were lots of cards submitted that were only a tiny bit different from an existing card, or the only thing interesting about them was a returning mechanic.
- If you're going to play in the deep end of the rules pool, you better know your stuff. Surprising number of entries this week with massive, functionality-breaking rules issues

<i>Public post-results spiel</i>

Chipmunk's Comments:
- This challenge put a lot of weird constraints on your design. The best entries were the ones that made those constraints seem easy, making cards that, if I didn't know about the prompts, I could easily imagine appearing as-is in a set and would enjoy playing with.
- Balance was a real struggle this time. There were commons that were maybe 2 mana overcosted, rares that would break upon contact with any decently creative combo player, and 5+ mana value bombs that exceed the payoff level that midrange decks should get for casting a 5- or 6-drop.
- I really like entries that include a new mechanic. It's a great way to show off more of the set that this card is hypothetically from. That said, if I see a mechanic, I'm imagining that this is a major mechanic of the set that shapes its limited environment and will have to play with other sets in constructed; make sure you're considering those additional implications.

<i>Public post-results spiel</i>

Chipmunk's Comments
- You know what I said about using a UB being risky, because the judge might not know about the thing you're referencing and be confused, or might just not like the thing you're referencing, or might like it but see it differently from you? Well that also goes for using a format other than limited or standard, and for the same reason.
- Some judges care about an entry holistically; how coherent it is, how it excites as a whole. Other judges are more focused on how each individual card will play. Good entries this week manaaged to satisfy both.
- There are certain effects that set off alarm bells in people experienced with constructed. Certains kinds of tutors. Certain kinds of mana cheating. Blinkable and recurrable things that should not be blinkable or recurrable. Between the judges, we've been burned by this stuff enough times to not want to play with fire again.

<b>Round 9:</b>
For this round, we're going back to where it all began--the Planeswalker Challenge. You'll design one planeswalker, one creature whose name references that planeswalker, and one instant or sorcery spell representing an impactful moment in that planeswalker's story.

Then, to celebrate the upcoming release of Reality Fracture, you'll do it again! For the same character, you'll design the same trio of cards from a different timeline. The second trio must share no colors with the first, and must be from a different set. The creatures must also represent different characters.

That is, in total, six cards:
Two planeswalkers representing the same character, in different colors across different timelines;
Two creatures with names that reference your planeswalker, representing different characters, also in different colors across different timelines;
and two instants/sorceries that represent flavorful moments from your planeswalker's story arc. It should go without saying, but also in different colors across different timelines.
And as a reminder, your two trios must be from different sets.

<i>Public post-results spiel</i>

Chipmunk's Comments:
- This round was very very close. I think every single one of these entries could have earned a 4 from me in any previous round. Really well done to all of you.
- That meant this came down to a lot of minor gripes and tiebreakers. One of the gripes that came up a lot was cards with very high Lion's Eye Quotient (Pip's term). I am not a fan of cards that have only one, unfair, use case that overshadows any other way you could use them.
- When comparing entries that made similar numbers of mistakes, what counted was the ability to impress someone, even if it wasn't me. The best entries made every card something that could impress someone, and the weaker entries had cards that didn't say anything new.

Congratulations on advancing to the season finale of MSE Survivor! Your finals entry will be building directly off of your semifinals submission--it's time to complete the Promised Year.
